Baptism in the Name of Jesus

Biblical foundation, the apostles' example, and the spiritual meaning of baptism by immersion.

August 18, 2026 • 9 min read

Water baptism is one of the most important steps in the life of a believer. The Bible teaches that it should be performed by immersion, invoking the name of Jesus Christ for the forgiveness of sins. But where does this practice come from, and why the name of Jesus specifically?

What is Christian Baptism?

The word "baptize" comes from the Greek baptizo, which means "to immerse" or "to submerge." Christian baptism is not a sprinkling or pouring of water, but a complete immersion — going under the water and coming back up — symbolizing death to the old life and resurrection to a new life in Christ.

"Know ye not, that so many of us as were baptized into Jesus Christ were baptized into his death? Therefore we are buried with him by baptism into death: that like as Christ was raised up from the dead by the glory of the Father, even so we also should walk in newness of life."
— Romans 6:3-4 (KJV)

The Command: Acts 2:38

On the Day of Pentecost, after the Holy Spirit fell upon the disciples, a crowd gathered and asked Peter what they should do. His answer established the pattern for the New Testament church:

"Then Peter said unto them, Repent, and be baptized every one of you in the name of Jesus Christ for the remission of sins, and ye shall receive the gift of the Holy Ghost."
— Acts 2:38 (KJV)

This verse gives us three clear commands: repent, be baptized in the name of Jesus Christ, and receive the Holy Spirit. This is the apostolic formula for salvation.

Five Baptisms in the Book of Acts

Every recorded baptism in the book of Acts was performed in the name of Jesus. There is no single instance of baptism using the titles "Father, Son, and Holy Spirit":

Passage Who was baptized Formula used
Acts 2:38 3,000 at Pentecost In the name of Jesus Christ
Acts 8:16 Samaritans In the name of the Lord Jesus
Acts 10:48 Cornelius' household (Gentiles) In the name of the Lord
Acts 19:5 Disciples at Ephesus In the name of the Lord Jesus
Acts 22:16 Paul (Saul of Tarsus) Calling on the name of the Lord

Why the Name of Jesus — Not Titles?

In Matthew 28:19, Jesus told His disciples: "Go ye therefore, and teach all nations, baptizing them in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Ghost."

Notice that Jesus said "in the name" (singular) — not "in the names." Father, Son, and Holy Ghost are not names; they are titles or roles. The name that encompasses all three is Jesus:

  • Father: Jesus said, "I am come in my Father's name" (John 5:43).
  • Son: The angel said, "thou shalt call his name JESUS" (Matthew 1:21).
  • Holy Ghost: Jesus said the Comforter would come "in my name" (John 14:26).

The apostles understood this perfectly, which is why every baptism they performed was in the name of Jesus — they were obeying Jesus' command in Matthew 28:19 by using the actual name.

The Spiritual Meaning of Baptism

Death and burial

Going under the water represents dying to sin and being buried with Christ (Romans 6:4, Colossians 2:12).

Resurrection to new life

Coming up out of the water represents rising with Christ to walk in newness of life (Romans 6:4).

Remission of sins

Baptism in Jesus' name is "for the remission of sins" — it washes away sin through faith (Acts 2:38, Acts 22:16).

Putting on Christ

"For as many of you as have been baptized into Christ have put on Christ" (Galatians 3:27).

Who Can Be Baptized?

The Bible teaches that baptism is for anyone who:

  • Believes in Jesus Christ as Lord and Savior (Mark 16:16, Acts 8:37).
  • Repents of their sins with a sincere heart (Acts 2:38).
  • Understands the significance of what they are doing — this is why infant baptism is not found in Scripture.

There are no age, gender, race, or social status requirements. The Ethiopian eunuch (Acts 8:36-38), the Roman centurion Cornelius (Acts 10:47-48), and the Philippian jailer (Acts 16:33) are all examples of people from diverse backgrounds who were baptized immediately upon believing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is baptism necessary for salvation?

Jesus said, "He that believeth and is baptized shall be saved" (Mark 16:16). Peter commanded it for the remission of sins (Acts 2:38). The Bible presents baptism not as optional, but as an essential part of God's plan of salvation working together with repentance and the Holy Spirit.

What if I was baptized differently before?

In Acts 19:1-5, Paul found disciples who had been baptized with John's baptism. When they learned the full truth, they were re-baptized in the name of the Lord Jesus. What matters is being baptized according to the apostolic pattern.

Does it have to be by immersion?

Yes. The Greek word baptizo means "to immerse." Every biblical example shows people going down into the water and coming up out of it (Acts 8:38-39, Matthew 3:16). Immersion is also the only form that properly symbolizes burial and resurrection (Romans 6:4).
